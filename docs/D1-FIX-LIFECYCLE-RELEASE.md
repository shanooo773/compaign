# D1 write fix and campaign lifecycle release - November 6, 2026

Production: https://ms-professor-agent-malang43.vercel.app/

1. **Exact cause.** `queueSequence()` in `apps/api/src/followup-sequence.ts` repeatedly selected every unqueued, uncancelled step with a NULL due date. When its original/previous email had not been sent, the timestamp subquery returned NULL. SQLite still executed an UPDATE from NULL to NULL. The row and the `followup_steps_due(queued,cancel_reason,due_at)` index incurred writes. This was schedule materialization write amplification, not storage exhaustion or email volume.

2. **Responsible SQL and measured counts.** The former statement was:

```sql
UPDATE followup_steps SET due_at=(
    SELECT CAST(round((julianday(base.sent_at)-2440587.5)*86400000) AS INTEGER)
    FROM messages base WHERE base.id=CASE WHEN followup_steps.relative_to='original' OR followup_steps.step=1
    THEN followup_steps.root_id ELSE (SELECT prev.child_id FROM followup_steps prev WHERE prev.root_id=followup_steps.root_id AND prev.step=followup_steps.step-1) END
    AND base.sent_at IS NOT NULL) + delay_value * CASE delay_unit WHEN 'minutes' THEN 60000 WHEN 'hours' THEN 3600000 ELSE 86400000 END
    WHERE due_at IS NULL AND queued=0 AND cancel_reason IS NULL
```

3. **Execution path.** Every-minute `scheduled()` retains its existing three-phase rotation. Phase zero calls reply synchronization, legacy follow-up queuing, and `queueSequence()`. Thus the faulty UPDATE could run approximately 480 times per day. Dashboard polling was not the dominant measured source. Heartbeats and reply checkpoints were much smaller contributors.

4. **Why two active users could consume the allowance.** Initial Insights averaged approximately 319 writes per materialization execution. At 480 executions this is approximately 153,120 writes/day for that statement alone, independent of new email submissions. Offline replay of the encrypted production snapshot matched 158 unchanged unresolved steps on every old execution. Index amplification explains why D1's write count exceeds the number of matched application rows.

5. **Worker errors.** The reported D1 daily-row-write-limit exceptions are consistent with this measured amplification. A complete retrospective breakdown of the earlier 32.3% error rate was not available, so it is not attributed entirely to Gmail or entirely to quota errors. A live observation of three natural scheduler executions after the critical fix recorded three successful outcomes and no exceptions. Public health checks pass.

6. **Changes.** The UPDATE now requires a prerequisite message with a valid `julianday(sent_at)` before selecting a missing due date. Existing non-NULL due dates, approved delays, ordering, content and schedules are unchanged. No schema migration was needed for the critical fix. Lifecycle additions use separate metadata tables and owner-scoped endpoints, indexed bounded cleanup, expiring deletion claims, idempotent completion audit, and conditional eligibility checks. Deleted, resolved workspaces stop reply polling so deleted notifications cannot reappear. Full-account erasure explicitly removes both approved follow-up steps and attachment-cleanup staging before their referenced rows.

7. **Before and after.** Offline production-snapshot replay: old query 158 matched rows on each of two executions; fixed query zero on each, retaining all 158 unresolved steps. An isolated 50-step fixture ran 480 fixed-query executions with zero row changes and verified correct one-time timestamp materialization when prerequisites became available. Production fixed-query measurements are in item 2. One steady observation before the lifecycle migration increased account writes by 57 over approximately 15.1 minutes, about 3.77 writes/minute: approximately 5,436/day if that small workload remained constant. This is an extrapolation, not a measured 24-hour total. Later account metrics include the one-time migration/index build and must not be extrapolated as idle steady-state usage. Fixed materialization reads averaged 348/execution, approximately 167,040/day at 480 executions; this excludes all other queries. Idle materialization now writes zero; scheduler health still normally writes two rows per tick, approximately 2,880/day.

8. **Cron.** `* * * * *` and the existing three-phase execution rotation are preserved. No manual production scheduler invocation, campaign test send, approval, cancellation, rescheduling, or acceleration was performed.

9. **Gmail correctness.** Existing OAuth credentials, token-refresh leases, folder-inclusive reply detection, Spam/Trash warnings, reply-based cancellation, rolling 50-submission allowance and exact 60-second pacing remain. Stable RFC Message-IDs and sent-folder reconciliation remain in place. Tests explicitly verify Gmail acceptance followed by a failed D1 receipt write is reconciled with one submission, and a quota-blocked cron stops before Gmail without retrying writes in a loop. No real email was sent during testing. The snapshot had zero unresolved transports, zero confirmed transports missing receipts, zero accepted attempts missing receipts and zero uncertain attempts. This is a database consistency check, not an independent historical Gmail Sent-folder audit.

10. **Protected production state.** Before and after deployment, read-only captures were encrypted with Windows CurrentUser DPAPI, decrypted for verification, and restored into local memory with integrity/foreign-key checks. Seven private attachment objects passed checksum/size verification. Protected-state comparisons passed with 13 campaign approvals, 37 pending initial emails, 188 unqueued uncancelled steps, 46 receipts, 44 attempts, 140 attachment associations, 116 attachment pins and all three connections intact. No message transitions occurred in the compared release windows. Private evidence is under ignored `apps/api/.wrangler`; no tokens, private email bodies or credentials appear in this report. Migration history was inspected: 0001 through 0015 initially; additive 0016 after release. The exact new schema and unchanged existing table rows were verified locally and against production.

11. **Quota result and safeguards.** The identified amplification is eliminated in the tested workload and in the measured production executions. Already consumed writes are not refunded. A full post-fix UTC day has not yet elapsed. `node scripts/monitor-d1-usage.cjs` reads account-wide Cloudflare analytics without writing D1, warns at 80,000 daily writes or an observed rate above 50/minute, and stores only local aggregate observations. It is an operator-run tool, not an always-running alert service. Existing scheduler first-write failure prevents sending while D1 is write-blocked; normal cron can recover after quota reset. No new paid service or plan change was introduced.

12. **Archive.** Archive Campaign is available on approved campaign details, with confirmation. The server verifies ownership and absence of pending initial emails, conditional follow-ups, leased/submitting transports and uncertain deliveries, then repeats eligibility in the mutation. It hides the campaign from the default directory while preserving approved content, receipts, replies, attachments and duplicate detection. The Archived filter exposes archived campaigns. Archiving frees no significant storage.

13. **Restore.** Restore Campaign removes the archive marker and restores normal listing. It does not reapprove or rearm cancelled work. Archived campaign history remains unchanged. Deleted workspaces cannot be restored.

14. **Permanent deletion.** Available for resolved completed/cancelled approved campaigns only. The red confirmation names the campaign and requires its exact name. Ownership, eligibility and confirmation are checked server-side. Expiring claims prevent simultaneous cleanup; completed requests are idempotent. Each request removes at most 100 attachment associations, 100 pins and 100 notifications, and queues at most 10 eligible documents. The interface continues bounded batches, with safe restart after interruption. It does not cancel an active campaign.

15. **Exactly what deletion removes and retains.** Under the user's selected retention policy, it removes campaign display-name overrides, campaign notifications, message-to-document associations and campaign attachment pins. The campaign is permanently removed from normal workspace listing through a deletion marker. Unreferenced private attachment objects are queued for the existing bounded cleaner; shared associations/pins prevent cleanup. Blob cleanup may take subsequent scheduler runs and retry on storage failure. Minimal deletion/progress metadata and document metadata remain. Immutable campaign approvals and selections, full approved message/authorization content, approved follow-up definitions/states, message/transport audit rows, deliveries, attempts, recipient/professor contact records, reply history, duplicate acknowledgments and audit events are retained. Gmail connections and Gmail messages remain. This is deliberately disclosed as permanent workspace deletion, not erasure of approved content or full account erasure. Storage reduction is principally unused attachment blobs, not the retained approved audit. Full account erasure remains separate and removes retained account data with its own confirmation and deferred opaque object cleanup.

16. **Duplicate-professor protection.** It survives both Archive and permanent workspace deletion because the immutable contact/sending evidence underlying `contacted_initials` is retained. Tests verify previous-contact warnings after deletion. Full account erasure is separately tested to remove the retained records rather than secretly keeping contact history.
